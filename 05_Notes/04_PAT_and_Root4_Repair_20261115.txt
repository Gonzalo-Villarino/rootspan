WHAT PAT DOES
Source: Villarino et al. (2024), PAT, doi:10.34133/plantphenomics.0156.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10981931/
The paper trained UNet++ with a ResNet encoder using9 slides/54 roots, cropped
into1471 patches. Training included flipped/rotated examples. Predicted patches
were stitched before rules connected periderm fragments and closed gaps.
Post-processing means changing predictions by explicit rules AFTER prediction;
it is not training and is not a new human annotation. Table2 shows that cleanup
did not improve every metric on every slide. Its benefits must be measured.
Our small teaching U-Net is not the published PAT architecture or weights.

IMPORTANT LIMITS AND SIMPLE CONCEPTS
Validation: checking a model version on a labelled example that is not used
for its training updates. Because we repeatedly use root4 to choose settings,
it is a development/validation example. It is not a final independent test.

Transfer learning: start with useful features learned earlier, then adapt them
using our own training examples. This is different from training from scratch.
The public PAT model's exposure to MT25_rep3 is UNKNOWN, so a PAT-derived
model cannot claim an independent test on this slide. A separately acquired
slide is needed later. Root6 remains reserved in our new training workflow.

Recall: of the pixels the human labelled as a category, how many received that
same prediction? It does not count all false positives. IoU also penalises
extra incorrectly predicted pixels. Background is very common; overall pixel
accuracy can look high even when the tissue distinction is poor.

Brightness/blur may matter, but these runs do not isolate image quality as the
cause. The archived root4 boundary is close to Gonzalo's revised boundary; that
does not justify moving the human reference to fit the model. The problematic
region is near the Periderm/Endoderm transition, not just a few boundary pixels.
